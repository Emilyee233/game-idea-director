# Worked Example: Beginner Concept → Playable Prototype

> Fictional example created for this Skill. It is not based on a private user project.

## Prompt
“I want to make a game about running a tiny observatory on a floating island, but I have never made a game and I do not know what the gameplay is.”

## Diagnosis
- Immediate objective: discover core gameplay.
- Relevant capability: user self-reports first project; do not infer all domains are Foundational.
- Primary reference: `core-gameplay.md`.
- Supporting reference: `scope-and-feasibility.md`.
- Do not begin with a full GDD.

## Three genuinely different directions

### A. Celestial Classification
Observe unusual skies, compare clues, identify phenomena, and choose what to record.
Engagement: inference and discovery.

### B. Instrument Operation
Manually align lenses, filters, and tracking equipment under changing conditions.
Engagement: execution, optimization, and mastery.

### C. Visitor Interpretation
Travelers bring strange sky stories; the player compares testimony with observations and decides what to believe or publish.
Engagement: narrative judgment and consequence.

## Recommendation
For a first prototype, test A because it can validate the central fantasy with low content and technical cost.

## Minimal prototype
- One night.
- Three observable phenomena.
- Two instruments.
- One notebook screen.
- Each phenomenon has several clues.
- Player chooses one classification and receives an explanation.

## Hypothesis
“Combining visual clues into a celestial classification creates satisfying inference.”

## What is not confirmed
Specific phenomena, progression, economy, characters, and long-term structure remain proposals.

## Success
The example demonstrates the Skill's behavior: theme → distinct gameplay options → recommendation → small falsifiable prototype.
