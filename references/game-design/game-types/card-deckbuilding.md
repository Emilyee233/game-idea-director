# Card & Deckbuilding Design

Use when cards, hands, decks, draws, drafting, deck construction, or card combinations are central.

## Design dimensions
- Card economy: draw, hand, discard, exhaust/remove, reshuffle, deck size.
- Action economy: energy, actions, tempo, turn structure, opportunity cost.
- Information: hidden/visible state and prediction.
- Card roles: damage, defense, setup, payoff, control, resource generation, utility.
- Synergy: interactions that create decisions rather than automatic multipliers.
- Consistency versus variance.
- Acquisition and removal.
- Encounter pressure.

## Never balance by efficiency alone
Damage-per-energy is one signal, not a verdict. Higher-cost cards may deserve an efficiency premium because they are less flexible, consume more of a turn, or create overkill risk. Low-cost cards may become stronger through repeated triggers or chains.

Before exact tuning consider total resource per turn, draw/hand constraints, deck size, card frequency, acquisition/rarity, damage thresholds, multipliers/statuses, action costs, and each card's intended role.

## Common failures
Strictly better cards; dominant archetypes; accidental infinite loops; randomness deciding outcomes before player decisions; large card pools before base combat decisions are proven.

## Prototype
Use a deliberately small card pool and encounter set. Separate formula comparison, simulation across decks/encounters, and human playtesting. Never declare cards balanced from isolated stat lines.
