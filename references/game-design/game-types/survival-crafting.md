# Survival, Crafting & Building Design

Do not assume survival requires hunger, thirst, temperature, crafting, base building, hostile creatures, or procedural worlds. Include only pressures that serve the intended experience.

## Design dimensions
- Pressure: what threatens or deteriorates?
- Resources: what is scarce, renewable, risky to obtain, or strategically reserved?
- Exploration: why leave safety?
- Transformation: how do resources become capability, shelter, knowledge, or access?
- Safety versus risk: what makes preparation meaningful?
- Building: if present, does it create safety, efficiency, expression, logistics, or another value?

## Resource-loop test
For each important resource ask where it comes from, what consumes it, what decision scarcity creates, what capability it unlocks, whether the player can become irrecoverably stuck, and whether gathering remains interesting.

## Common failures
Maintenance meters become chores; crafting has many recipes but few choices; exploration rewards are interchangeable; scarcity creates waiting rather than planning.

## Prototype
Use one compact area, a small resource graph, one meaningful threat, and one capability upgrade. Test whether leaving safety and returning more capable creates worthwhile decisions.
