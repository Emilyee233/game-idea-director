# Scope and Feasibility

## Purpose

Help users preserve the value of their game idea while choosing a scope that matches actual resources and capabilities.

## Assess five areas

1. **Technical:** engine, platform, architecture, networking, simulation, performance, persistence, and integration.
2. **Art and content:** asset volume, uniqueness, animation, variation, audio, writing, levels, and ongoing content needs.
3. **Team and skills:** available people, relevant experience, AI assistance, review capacity, and specialist gaps.
4. **Time and budget:** known constraints, external costs, human effort, agent runtime, review, integration, and rework.
5. **Design and maintenance complexity:** system interactions, data volume, balancing burden, testability, and future changes.

Do not invent precise estimates or success probabilities. State assumptions, unknowns, and confidence. Use ranges when reasonable and explain what they depend on.

## Scope reduction

1. Identify the experience the user most wants to preserve.
2. Identify the mechanics required to deliver or test it.
3. Separate essential, optional, conditional, and deferred work for the chosen milestone.
4. Identify dependencies and expensive risks.
5. Propose a smaller scope that still tests the central value.
6. Explain consequences of removing or delaying features.
7. Obtain approval before changing confirmed scope.

Do not remove a defining feature merely because it is expensive. Consider a cheaper representation or smaller test first.

## Milestone distinctions

- **Concept experiment:** tests a specific assumption or interaction.
- **Playable prototype:** lets someone experience and test the core gameplay in a limited form.
- **Vertical slice:** demonstrates a representative portion at near-target production quality.
- **Full game:** the complete intended product.

Do not conflate these milestones or imply that a prototype is a finished game.

## Estimation

Estimate by deliverable and work type: design, art/content, programming, integration, testing, review, and rework/risk buffer.

Distinguish human work time, agent execution time, waiting time, and review time. AI generation does not eliminate integration, quality control, or maintenance.

If information is insufficient, identify the missing inputs rather than fabricating an estimate.

## Risk review

Identify major uncertainties and recommend the cheapest useful validation. A high-risk technical or gameplay assumption may deserve a small experiment before extensive content production.

## Feasibility assessment

When useful, summarize:
- Project intent and proposed milestone.
- Known team, time, budget, and platform constraints.
- Feasibility observations by area.
- Assumptions and unknowns.
- Highest risks.
- Features to preserve, simplify, or defer.
- Recommended next experiment or milestone.

Use qualitative conclusions such as **currently plausible**, **needs scope reduction**, or **requires an experiment first**. Explain the basis. Do not calculate an unsupported single score or predict commercial success.
