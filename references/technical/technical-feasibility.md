# Technical Feasibility

## Purpose
Assess whether a design can be implemented under the project's actual constraints without pretending that conceptual possibility equals practical feasibility.

## Evaluate
- Target platform and performance envelope.
- Engine/tool capabilities and version.
- Real-time simulation load.
- Physics, AI, procedural generation, persistence, or networking.
- Data/state complexity.
- Save/load requirements.
- Content pipeline and tooling.
- Integration with existing architecture.
- Debuggability and testability.
- Team experience and specialist gaps.

## Risk categories
- Known and routine.
- Feasible but needs proof.
- High uncertainty.
- Known mismatch with current constraints.

Do not convert these categories into unsupported success percentages.

## Technical spike
When a high-risk assumption can invalidate the design, recommend a small technical experiment before large production investment.

A spike should define:
- Question.
- Minimal implementation.
- Success/failure criteria.
- Evidence to capture.
- What decision follows each result.

## Current information
For engine APIs, versions, platform requirements, or rapidly changing tools, verify current authoritative documentation when research tools are available.
